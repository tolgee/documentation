Community Translation is a Tolgee feature for projects that want translation help from their
community, for example open-source localization by volunteers. Open a project to community
translation and anyone can browse it, even without an account. Anyone with a Tolgee account can
suggest translations and leave comments. Contributors cannot edit anything directly. Project
members review the suggestions and accept the ones they like.

The page has two parts. The first is [for project owners and members](/platform/translation_process/community-translation#for-project-owners-and-members): opening a project,
sharing it, and reviewing what comes in. The second is [for contributors](/platform/translation_process/community-translation#for-contributors): what
you can do on a community project and how to start.

## For Project Owners and Members

As an owner you decide whether a project is open to the community, and as a reviewer you decide
which of the contributors' suggestions go live. Community Translation is not gated by a plan. It
is available on Tolgee Cloud and on self-hosted Tolgee v3.215.0 or newer.

### Opening a Project to Community Translation {#making-a-project-public}

Opening a project turns on Community Translation for it. A project that is open is called a
**community project**. Only the **organization owner** (or a server administrator on a
self-hosted instance) can open a project, because it shows the project to everyone on the
internet.

Before you open the project, know what you expose.

- Anyone, with or without an account, can read every translation and screenshot in every
  language. Anyone with a Tolgee account can also export the whole project.
- Anyone can see who on your team translated, reviewed or commented, and each of those members
  gets a public [community profile](./community-profile.mdx) unless they hide it.

Do not open a project whose strings must stay private, and tell your team first.

To open the project, follow these steps.

1. Open `Project settings` and switch to the `Advanced` tab.
2. In the `Community translation` section, turn on `Open this project to community translation`.
3. Confirm the dialog. Tolgee may then ask for your password.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/public-toggle.webp"
  alt="Community translation section at the top of the Advanced tab in project settings, with the Open this project to community translation switch turned on and highlighted, and the Community project page box under it"
/>

Opening the project also turns on
[`Suggestions mode`](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions#how-to-enable-suggestions),
because contributors can only suggest while it is on. If the switch does not react to your
click, you are neither the organization owner nor a server administrator. Ask an owner to open
the project for you.

On a self-hosted instance, browsing without an account needs
[`tolgee.authentication.enabled`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-enabled),
and contributors can create accounts only with
[`tolgee.authentication.registrations-allowed`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-registrations-allowed).
Both are off in a default installation. An invitation would make the person a member, not a
contributor.

:::info
If your project is open source and non-commercial, Tolgee offers a free Optimise plan for it.
Write to [sales@tolgee.io](mailto:sales@tolgee.io) with a link to the project.
:::

### Setting Up the Community Project Page

Every community project has a **community project page**, the first thing a visitor sees. Set
it up so that newcomers know what the project is and how you want them to help. Only the
organization owner or a server administrator can change it.

Right after you open a project, Tolgee walks you through four optional steps: a description of
the project, your rules for contributors, a link and a badge to share, and a banner for websites
that support [in-context translation](./community-translation-in-context.mdx).

<ScreenshotWrapper

To change any of it later, use the `Community project page` box under the switch. `View page` in
the box opens the page as visitors see it.

### Sharing the Project with Your Community

Contributors find your project in the community projects list, or through a link you give them.

- The list of all community projects is at
  [`https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects`](https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects). On a
  self-hosted instance, it is at the same path on your own address.
- The link to your community project page, and a badge for your README or website, are under
  `Share it` in the [`Community project page` box](#setting-up-the-community-project-page).

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/public-projects.webp"
  alt="Community projects list seen without an account, with the Community translations banner, one community project row, and the Log in and Sign up buttons"
/>

If your website uses the Tolgee JS SDK, contributors can also suggest translations directly on
the site. Read more in [Community Translation in Context](./community-translation-in-context.mdx).

### Reviewing Community Suggestions

Contributors propose changes as suggestions, so nothing they submit goes live until a reviewer
accepts it. Accepting needs permission to both edit translations and change their state in the
language. Declining needs only the permission to change their state. The `Review` role has
both.

Open the translation in the editor. When it has several suggestions, you have two ways to accept
one of them.

- The check mark icon, with the tooltip `Accept only`, accepts the suggestion and leaves the
  others open.
- `Accept and decline others`, in the suggestion's `⋮` menu, accepts it and dismisses the rest.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/suggestions-accept.webp"
  alt="Two community suggestions on a translation with the Accept only check mark and the Accept and decline others menu action highlighted"
/>

To see who is behind a suggestion, hover over the author's avatar next to it.

For the full suggestion workflow, read
[Translation Suggestions](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions).

### Seeing Who Has Contributed

Tolgee keeps track of the people who help with a community project, so you can recognize them
and invite the most active ones to your team. You find them in two places.

- The contributors page is public and counts everyone's work, your team's included. Open it
  with `All contributors` on the community project page.
- The `Community` tab on the project's
  [Members](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#community-contributors) page lists the people who are not members. It appears once the project has its first
  contributor.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/contributors-page.webp"

To make a frequent contributor a full member, use `Invite to Team` on their row in the
`Community` tab. You need the permission to manage members.

### Closing a Project to the Community {#making-a-project-private-again}

Close a project when you no longer want help from outside your team, or when its strings have to
stop being visible to everyone. The organization owner or a server administrator can close it.

1. Open `Project settings` and switch to the `Advanced` tab.
2. In the `Community translation` section, turn off `Open this project to community translation`.
3. Confirm the dialog. Tolgee may then ask for your password.

People who are not members lose access. The suggestions and comments they made stay in the
project, but the project's work disappears from their
[community profiles](./community-profile.mdx) until you open it again.

## For Contributors

As a contributor you help translate a project you are not a member of. You can look around
without an account. To suggest or comment, you need a Tolgee account on the server the project
lives on, and nothing else: no invitation, no seat, and no approval from the project's owner.

### What Contributors Can Do

What you can do on a community project depends on whether you are logged in.

- **Without an account**, you can read the translations and screenshots in all languages, and
  see who contributed. You cannot export.
- **With a Tolgee account**, you get **community permissions**. You can also propose
  [translation suggestions](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions),
  [comment](./comments.mdx) on translations, delete your own suggestions, and export.

Nobody outside the team can change an existing translation or its
[state](./translation_states.mdx). Contributors are not project members and do not use a seat.
To edit translations directly, you have to be
[invited as a member](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#how-to-add-members-to-your-project).

### Starting to Translate a Community Project

The community project page is where you pick a language and start. You do not need an account to
open it.

1. Open the [community projects list](https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects) and click a project,
   or follow a link from the project's website or README.
2. Read `How to contribute`, then click a language.
3. Log in, or sign up if you do not have an account yet.
4. Click a translation, type your version, and click `Suggest`.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/community-project-page.webp"
  alt="Community project page seen without an account, with the project description, the list of languages with progress bars and words left, the How to contribute text, the Translate right in the app banner, and the top contributors"
/>

When the page shows the `Translate right in the app` banner, you can also suggest directly in
the project's app, as described in
[Translating a Website with Tolgee](../translate-with-tolgee.mdx). Your work in community
projects adds up on your [community profile](./community-profile.mdx).

### Finding Projects You Have Contributed To

Once you have suggested or commented on a community project, Tolgee keeps a list of the projects
you have helped with.

1. Log in to Tolgee. You land on your projects list.
2. Open the organization switcher, the drop-down next to the `Projects` heading.
3. Pick `Community translation` at the bottom of the list.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/community-projects.webp"
  alt="Projects list with the organization switcher set to Community translation and the My contributions only toggle turned on"
/>

Turn off `My contributions only` to browse every community project.
