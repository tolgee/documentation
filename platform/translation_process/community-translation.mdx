Community Translation is a Tolgee feature for projects that want translation help from their
community, for example open-source localization by volunteers. Open a project to community
translation and anyone can browse it, even without an account. Anyone with a Tolgee account can
suggest translations and leave comments. Contributors cannot edit anything directly. Project
members review the suggestions and accept the ones they like.

The page has two parts. The first is [for project owners and members](/platform/translation_process/community-translation#for-project-owners-and-members): opening a project,
setting up its community project page, sharing it, reviewing suggestions, and seeing who
contributed. The second is [for contributors](/platform/translation_process/community-translation#for-contributors): what
you can do on a community project and where to find the projects you have helped with.

## For Project Owners and Members

As an owner you decide whether a project is open to the community, and as a reviewer you decide
which of the contributors' suggestions go live. Community Translation is not gated by a plan. It
is available on Tolgee Cloud and on self-hosted Tolgee v3.215.0 or newer.

### Opening a Project to Community Translation {#making-a-project-public}

Opening a project is what turns on Community Translation for it. A project that is open to
community translation is called a **community project**. Only the **organization owner** (or a
server administrator on a self-hosted instance) can open a project, because it shows the project
to everyone on the internet.

Before you open the project, know what you expose. Anyone, with or without an account, can read
every translation and screenshot in every language. Anyone with a Tolgee account can also export
the whole project. Do not open a project whose strings must stay private.

Opening the project also makes your team's work in it visible. Anyone can see who translated,
reviewed, commented or suggested, and the public contributors page lists team members next to
outside contributors. Each member who has worked on translations in the project gets a public
[community profile](./community-profile.mdx#who-can-see-a-community-profile), unless they hide
it. Tell your team before you open the project.

To open the project, follow these steps.

1. Open `Project settings` and switch to the `Advanced` tab.
2. In the `Community translation` section, turn on `Open this project to community translation`.
3. Confirm the `Open to community translation?` dialog. Because this is a sensitive change, Tolgee
   may then ask for your password.

Three things happen when you confirm.

- The project appears in the [community projects list](https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects), where anyone can find and browse it.
- Tolgee turns on [`Suggestions mode`](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions#how-to-enable-suggestions) for the project, because contributors can only suggest
  while it is on. If you turn it off later, nobody can suggest until you turn it on again.
- The `Set up your community page` dialog opens, and the `Community project page` box appears
  under the switch. Both are described in
  [Setting Up the Community Project Page](#setting-up-the-community-project-page).

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/public-toggle.webp"
  alt="Community translation section at the top of the Advanced tab in project settings, with the Open this project to community translation switch turned on and the Community project page box under it"
/>

Contributors do not become members and cannot edit existing translations.

If clicking the `Open this project to community translation` switch does nothing, and its
tooltip says `Only the organization owner can change this.`, you are neither the organization
owner nor a server administrator. Ask an owner to open the project for you.

On a self-hosted instance, contributors need accounts on your server, and they create them
themselves. A default installation has authentication turned off and registrations closed, so
turn on [`tolgee.authentication.enabled`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-enabled)
and [`tolgee.authentication.registrations-allowed`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-registrations-allowed).
Browsing without an account also needs authentication turned on. An invitation would make the
person a member, not a contributor.

:::info
If your project is open source and non-commercial, Tolgee offers a free Optimise plan for it.
Write to [sales@tolgee.io](mailto:sales@tolgee.io) with a link to the project.
:::

### Setting Up the Community Project Page

Every community project has a **community project page**. It is the first thing a visitor sees:
your description, the languages with their progress, your rules for contributors, and the top
contributors. Setting it up tells newcomers what the project is and how you want them to help.
Only the organization owner or a server administrator can change the page.

Right after you open a project, Tolgee shows the `Set up your community page` dialog. It has four
steps, and every step is optional.

1. `Project description` introduces the project in up to 10,000 characters of Markdown. While it
   is empty, the page shows the description from the project's general settings.
2. `How to contribute` holds your rules and tips, for example the tone or the terms to use, in up
   to 10,000 characters of Markdown. While it is empty, the page shows a short default text.
3. `Share it` gives you the `Project page link` and a `Badge` to copy. They are described in
   [Sharing the Project with Your Community](#sharing-the-project-with-your-community).
4. `In-context translation` is for web apps that use the Tolgee JS SDK. Turn on
   `Show on public page` to add a `Translate right in the app` banner to the page. Fill in
   `Link to your app (optional)` to add an `Open app` link to the banner. The link must be an
   `http` or `https` address.

`Next` saves the step and opens the following one, `Skip` moves on without filling the step in,
and `Finish later` closes the dialog. Tolgee saves what you have entered each time you leave a
step.

<ScreenshotWrapper

The dialog opens only right after you open the project. To change the page later, use the
`Community project page` box in `Project settings` > `Advanced`, under the
`Open this project to community translation` switch. The box lists
the same four items, with a check mark next to the ones that are filled in. The pencil icon on
each row opens that item for editing, and `View page` opens the community project page. The
settings are kept when you close the project to the community.

### Sharing the Project with Your Community

Contributors can reach your project in three ways. Use the ones that fit where your community
already is.

- **The community projects list** shows every community project. Clicking a project opens its
  community project page.
- **A link or a badge** takes people from your README or website straight to the community
  project page.
- **Your own website** can accept suggestions directly, if it uses the Tolgee JS SDK. Read more in
  [Community Translation in Context](./community-translation-in-context.mdx).

The community projects list is at
[`https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects`](https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects). On a
self-hosted instance, the list is at the same path on your own address, for example
`https://tolgee.example.com/public-projects`. The list opens with a `Community translations`
banner that explains to newcomers what they can do. Projects with the most keys come first, and
projects with the same number of keys are sorted by name.

You find the link and the badge under `Share it` in the
[`Community project page` box](#setting-up-the-community-project-page). `Project page link` is
the address of the page, for example `https://app.tolgee.io/projects/1234/community`. `Badge` is
a `Translate with Tolgee` badge that links to the page, as `Markdown` for a README or as `HTML`
for a website. `Mark as shared` only records for your team that you have shared the project.

Anyone can browse the list and the project without an account. To suggest or comment, a
contributor logs in, or creates a free account on Tolgee Cloud.

### Reviewing Community Suggestions

Contributors propose changes as suggestions, never as direct edits, so nothing they submit goes
live until a reviewer accepts it. Accepting needs permission to both edit translations and change
their state in the language, and declining needs only the permission to change their state. The
`Review` role has both. Open the translation in the editor and review the incoming
suggestions the same way you review any other suggestion.

A translation with a single suggestion has one way to accept it: the check mark icon on the
suggestion, with the tooltip `Accept suggestion`. When a translation has several suggestions, you
have two ways to accept one of them.

- The check mark icon on the suggestion, with the tooltip `Accept only`, promotes that suggestion
  to the live translation and leaves the other suggestions open, which is useful when you want to
  keep comparing alternatives.
- `Accept and decline others`, in the suggestion's `⋮` menu, promotes the suggestion and dismisses
  the competing ones in a single step.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/suggestions-accept.webp"
  alt="Two community suggestions on a translation with the Accept only check mark and the Accept and decline others menu action"
/>

On a community project, you can check who is behind a suggestion before you decide. Hover over
the author's avatar next to the suggestion to open a card. Everything on the card is about this project: how many suggestions the author
has made, how many were accepted, how many contributions of any kind the author has, and when the
author was last active. When
the author's [community profile](./community-profile.mdx) is public, the card also has a
`View profile` link.

### Seeing Who Has Contributed

Tolgee keeps track of the people who help with a community project. This lets you recognize them
and invite the most active ones to join your team. You find them in two places.

- **The contributors page** is public and lists everyone who worked on translations, team
  members included.
- **The `Community` tab** of the Members page is for your team and lists only the people who are
  not members.

To open the contributors page, click `All contributors` on the community project page. The page
shows an activity chart and one card for each person, with their numbers of suggestions, accepted
suggestions and contributions. A contribution is a translation, a review, a comment or a
suggestion made by hand. You can change the period and the languages, and sort the cards, for
example by `Most accepted`. A card links to the person's
[community profile](./community-profile.mdx) when the profile is public.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/contributors-page.webp"
  alt="Contributors page of a community project with the period and language filters, the activity chart, and contributor cards showing suggestions, accepted suggestions and contributions"
/>

The `Community` tab is on the project's
[Members](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members) page, and you need the permission to view
members to see it. As soon as a community project has its first contributor, the page splits
into two tabs.

- The `Team` tab lists your project members, as it always did.
- The `Community` tab lists everyone with activity on the project who is not currently a member,
  with the dates of their first and last contribution. A former member who edited translations
  while still a member appears here too.

The name of each contributor in the `Community` tab links to their community profile.

If you want a frequent contributor to become a full member, for example so they can edit
translations directly, use the `Invite to Team` button on their row in the `Community` tab. This
sends them the same
[project invitation](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#how-to-add-members-to-your-project)
you would send anyone else. You need the permission to manage members to invite a contributor.

### Closing a Project to the Community {#making-a-project-private-again}

Close a project when you no longer want help from outside your team, or when its strings have to
stop being visible to everyone. The same people who can open a project can close it: the
organization owner or a server administrator.

To close the project, follow these steps.

1. Open `Project settings` and switch to the `Advanced` tab.
2. In the `Community translation` section, turn off `Open this project to community translation`.
3. Confirm the `Close community translation?` dialog. Tolgee may then ask for your password.

The project disappears from the community projects list, and people who are not members lose
access to it. Suggestions and comments they already made stay in the project, and
`Suggestions mode` stays as it is. The `Community` tab on the Members page is hidden while the
project is closed.

Closing the project also removes it from the
[community profiles](./community-profile.mdx) of everyone who worked on it. Their counts and
activity from the project are no longer shown, and they return if you open the project again.

## For Contributors

As a contributor you help translate a project you are not a member of. You can look around
without an account. To suggest or comment, you need a Tolgee account on the server the project
lives on, and nothing else: no invitation, no seat, and no approval from the project's owner.

### What Contributors Can Do

What you can do on a community project depends on whether you are logged in. Without an account,
you browse as a guest. A guest can

- View translations and screenshots in **all languages**
- View the community project page, the contributors page, and public
  [community profiles](./community-profile.mdx)
- View the translation activity of the project: who translated, reviewed, commented or suggested

A guest cannot export translations or see deleted keys. Clicking a translation as a guest opens
the `Log in to contribute` dialog.

Every user with a Tolgee account gets **community permissions** on a community project. These are
a limited subset of the [`View` role](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#permissions)
plus the ability to suggest and comment. On top of what a guest can do, a logged-in contributor can

- Propose [translation suggestions](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions), if the project has suggestions enabled
- [Comment](./comments.mdx) on translations, for example to flag a wrong translation
- Delete their own suggestions
- Export translations

Community permissions never let anyone change an existing translation or change its
[state](./translation_states.mdx) (translated or reviewed).

Contributors are **not** project members. They do not use a seat, and opening a project does
not affect its billing. To allow someone to edit translations directly, the project has to
[invite them as a member](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#how-to-add-members-to-your-project).

### Starting to Translate a Community Project

The community project page is where you pick a language and start. You do not need an account to
open it. To get from the page to your first suggestion, follow these steps.

1. Open the [community projects list](https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects) and click a project,
   or follow a link from the project's website or README.
2. Read `How to contribute` on the community project page. It holds the rules the project's
   owners want you to follow.
3. Click a language in the list. Each language shows how much is translated and how many words
   are left. The translations open with the project's base language next to the language you
   picked.
4. Log in, or sign up if you do not have an account yet. As a guest, you see the bar
   `You are browsing as a guest. Log in or sign up to suggest translations.` above the
   translations.
5. Click a translation, type your version, and click `Suggest`.

<ScreenshotWrapper

The page also lets you search and sort the languages. Its side column lists the
`Top contributors` and the `Project details`, such as the number of words to translate. Once you
have contributed to a language, the page marks it with a star and keeps it at the top of the
list. When the page shows the `Translate right in the app` banner, you can also suggest
directly in the project's app. `How it works` in the banner opens
[Translating a Website with Tolgee](../translate-with-tolgee.mdx).

Your suggestions, and the comments and translations you make in community projects, add up on
your [community profile](./community-profile.mdx).

### Finding Projects You Have Contributed To

Once you have suggested or commented on at least one community project, Tolgee gives you a page
that lists the projects you have helped with. To open it, follow these steps.

1. Log in to Tolgee. You land on your projects list.
2. Open the organization switcher, the drop-down next to the `Projects` heading, which normally
   lists your organizations.
3. Pick `Community translation` at the bottom of the list.

The page that opens is the community projects list, shown inside your account. The
`My contributions only` toggle in its top right corner narrows the list to the projects you have
suggested or commented on. It is on when you open the page from the switcher. Turn it off to
browse every community project.
