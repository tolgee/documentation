Community Translation is a Tolgee feature for projects that want translation help from their
community, for example open-source localization by volunteers. Make a project public and anyone
with a Tolgee account can browse it, suggest translations, and leave comments. Contributors cannot
edit anything directly. Project members review the suggestions and accept the ones they like. You
never need to invite volunteers as members.

The page has two parts. The first is for project owners and members: making a project public,
sharing it, reviewing suggestions, and seeing who contributed. The second is for contributors: what
you can do on a public project and where to find the projects you have helped with.

## For Project Owners and Members

As an owner you decide whether a project is public, and as a reviewer you decide which of the
contributors' suggestions go live. Community Translation is not gated by a plan. It
is available on Tolgee Cloud and on self-hosted Tolgee v3.215.0 or newer.

### Making a Project Public

Publishing a project is what turns on Community Translation for it. Only the **organization
owner** (or a server administrator on a self-hosted instance) can change this, because it opens
the project to anyone with a Tolgee account.

Before you publish, know what you expose. Contributors get the same view access as a member with
the `View` role: they can read every translation and screenshot in every language, and they can
export the whole project. Do not publish a project whose strings must stay private.

To publish the project, follow these steps.

1. Open `Project settings` and switch to the `Advanced` tab.
2. In the `Public project` section, turn on `Make this project public`.
3. Confirm the `Make project public?` dialog. Because this is a sensitive change, Tolgee may then
   ask for your password.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/public-toggle.webp"
  alt="Public project section at the top of the Advanced tab in project settings, with the Make this project public toggle turned on"
/>

From then on, anyone with a Tolgee account can find the project on the public projects page and
suggest translations, as long as [suggestions are enabled](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions#how-to-enable-suggestions).
Contributors do not become members and cannot edit existing translations.

If you cannot see the `Public project` section or the switch is disabled, you are neither the
organization owner nor a server administrator. Ask an owner to publish the project for you.

On a self-hosted instance, contributors need accounts on your server, and they create them
themselves. A default installation has authentication turned off and registrations closed, so
turn on [`tolgee.authentication.enabled`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-enabled)
and [`tolgee.authentication.registrations-allowed`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-registrations-allowed).
An invitation would make the person a member, not a contributor.

:::info
If your project is open source and non-commercial, Tolgee offers a free Business plan for it.
Write to [sales@tolgee.io](mailto:sales@tolgee.io) with a link to the project.
:::

### Sharing the Project with Your Community

Public projects are listed on the public projects page at
[`https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects`](https://app.tolgee.io/public-projects). On a self-hosted
instance, the page is at the same path on your own address, for example
`https://tolgee.example.com/public-projects`. Share that link, or the link of your project, with
the people you want to help.

The page opens with a `Community translations` banner that explains to newcomers what they can do,
followed by the list of public projects. Anyone can browse it without an account. To suggest or
comment, a contributor logs in, or creates a free account on Tolgee Cloud.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/public-projects.webp"
  alt="Public projects page seen while logged out, with the Community translations banner and one public project row"
/>

Contributors do not have to work inside Tolgee. If your website uses the Tolgee JS SDK, they can
suggest translations directly on the site with the browser plugin. Read more in
[Community Translation in Context](./community-translation-in-context.mdx).

### Reviewing Community Suggestions

Contributors propose changes as suggestions, never as direct edits, so nothing they submit goes
live until a reviewer accepts it. Accepting needs permission to both edit translations and change
their state in the language, and declining needs only the permission to change their state. The
`Review` role has both. Open the translation in the editor and review the incoming
suggestions the same way you review any other suggestion.

When a suggestion is good, you have two ways to accept it.

- The check mark icon on the suggestion, with the tooltip `Accept only`, promotes that suggestion
  to the live translation and leaves the other suggestions open, which is useful when you want to
  keep comparing alternatives.
- `Accept and decline others`, in the suggestion's `⋮` menu, promotes the suggestion and dismisses
  the competing ones in a single step.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/suggestions-accept.webp"
  alt="Two community suggestions on a translation with the Accept only check mark and the Accept and decline others menu action"
/>

For the full suggestion workflow, including how to enable suggestions for your project, read
[Translation Suggestions](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions).

### Seeing Who Has Contributed

When someone who is not a member suggests or comments on your public project, Tolgee records them
as a **contributor**. This lets you recognize the people helping out and invite the most active
ones to join your team.

Contributors appear on the project's [Members](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members) page.
As soon as a public project has its first contributor, the page splits into two tabs.

- The `Team` tab lists your project members, as it always did.
- The `Community` tab lists everyone with activity on the project who is not currently a member,
  with the dates of their first and last contribution. A former member who edited translations
  while still a member appears here too.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/members-community-tab.webp"
  alt="Community tab on the Members page listing contributors with contribution dates and an Invite to Team button"
/>

If you want a frequent contributor to become a full member, for example so they can edit
translations directly, use the `Invite to Team` button on their row. This sends them the same
[project invitation](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#how-to-add-members-to-your-project)
you would send anyone else. You need the permission to manage members to invite a contributor.

### Making a Project Private Again

Make a project private again when you no longer want help from outside your team, or when its
strings have to stop being visible to everyone. The same people who can publish a project can make
it private: the organization owner or a server administrator.

To make the project private, follow these steps.

1. Open `Project settings` and switch to the `Advanced` tab.
2. In the `Public project` section, turn off `Make this project public`.
3. Confirm the `Make project private?` dialog. Tolgee may then ask for your password.

The project disappears from the public projects page, and people who are not members lose access to
it. Suggestions and comments they already made stay in the project. The `Community` tab on the
Members page is hidden while the project is private.

## For Contributors

As a contributor you help translate a project you are not a member of. You need a Tolgee account
on the server the project lives on, and nothing else: no invitation, no seat, and no approval from
the project's owner.

### What Contributors Can Do

When a project is public, every user with a Tolgee account gets **community permissions** on it.
These are a limited subset of the [`View` role](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#permissions)
plus the ability to suggest and comment. A contributor can

- View and export translations and screenshots in **all languages**
- View the project activity
- Propose [translation suggestions](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions), if the project has suggestions enabled
- [Comment](./comments.mdx) on translations, for example to flag a wrong translation
- Delete their own suggestions

Community permissions never let anyone change an existing translation or change its
[state](./translation_states.mdx) (translated or reviewed).

Contributors are **not** project members. They do not use a seat, and publishing a project does
not affect its billing. To allow someone to edit translations directly, the project has to
[invite them as a member](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#how-to-add-members-to-your-project).

### Finding Projects You Have Contributed To

Once you have suggested or commented on at least one public project, Tolgee gives you a page that
lists the projects you have helped with. To open it, follow these steps.

1. Log in to Tolgee. You land on your projects list.
2. Open the organization switcher, the drop-down next to the `Projects` heading, which normally
   lists your organizations.
3. Pick `Community translation` at the bottom of the list.

The community projects page is the public projects page shown inside your account. The
`My contributions only` toggle in its top right corner narrows the list to the projects you have
suggested or commented on. It is on when you open the page from the switcher. Turn it off to
browse every public project.
