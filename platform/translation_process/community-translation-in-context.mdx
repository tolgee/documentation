Community Translation in Context lets contributors suggest translations on your live website
instead of inside Tolgee. It is for owners of public projects whose site uses the Tolgee JS SDK.
A contributor installs the [Tolgee Tools browser plugin](/js-sdk/in-context#tolgee-browser-plugin), signs in with their own Tolgee account,
Alt+clicks a string on your site, and proposes a better translation. You review the suggestion in
Tolgee, and nothing changes on the site until you accept it.

This page is for the project owner: what to switch on in the project, what to declare on the
website, and what contributors can and cannot do in the dialog. If you came here to translate
someone else's website, read [Translating a Website with Tolgee](../translate-with-tolgee.mdx)
instead.

A [public project](./community-translation.mdx) lets anyone with a Tolgee account suggest
translations, but only inside the Tolgee platform. The contributor has to find your project, find
the right key, and guess where the string appears in your product. Most people who notice a clumsy
translation on your website never get that far. With in-context suggesting, a Czech user of your
open-source app who notices that a checkout button says "Zaplatit nyní" where "Zaplatit" would
read better can Alt+click the button, type the new text, and click `Suggest`. The next time you
open the key in Tolgee, the suggestion is waiting for you.

## Preparing Your Project

Contributors can only suggest when the project is open to them and accepts suggestions. Both
switches are in `Project settings` > `Advanced`.

1. [Make the project public](./community-translation.mdx#making-a-project-public).
2. Turn on [`Suggestions mode`](/platform/projects_and_organizations/suggestions#how-to-enable-suggestions).
   While it is off, nobody can create a suggestion, in the platform, in context, or through the
   API.

On Tolgee Cloud, that is all. A self-hosted instance needs three more things before contributors
can sign in through the plugin.

- Tolgee v3.230.0 or newer.
- The browser extension [turned on](/platform/self_hosting/oauth_sign_in#turn-the-browser-extension-on)
  with `tolgee.oauth2.browser-extension-enabled`, together with `tolgee.front-end-url` set to your
  public address. With the extension enabled, Tolgee refuses to start until it knows its own
  address.
- Open sign-up, so contributors can create their own accounts. A default installation has
  authentication off and registrations closed, so turn on
  [`tolgee.authentication.enabled`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-enabled)
  and
  [`tolgee.authentication.registrations-allowed`](/platform/self_hosting/configuration#tolgee-authentication-_-registrations-allowed).

## Preparing Your Website

The browser plugin has to know which Tolgee server and which project your website belongs to. It
reads both from the configuration of the [Tolgee JS SDK](/js-sdk) on the page, so declare
[`apiUrl`](/js-sdk/api/core_package/options#apiurl) and
[`projectId`](/js-sdk/api/core_package/options#projectid) in your production build. Without
`projectId`, the plugin can't offer signing in on your site, and contributors could only connect
with an API key, which they don't have.

```ts
import { Tolgee, FormatSimple } from '@tolgee/web';

const tolgee = Tolgee()
  .use(FormatSimple())
  .init({
    language: 'en',
    apiUrl: 'https://app.tolgee.io',
    projectId: 1234,
    staticData: {
      en: () => import('./i18n/en.json'),
      cs: () => import('./i18n/cs.json'),
    },
  });
```

You find the project ID in the URL of your project in Tolgee, for example
`https://app.tolgee.io/projects/1234`.

:::caution
Don't add an `apiKey` to your production configuration. It isn't needed here. Each contributor
signs in with their own Tolgee account through the plugin, and Tolgee decides what they can do
from that account.
:::

You don't need to ship the in-context tools to every visitor either. The plugin adds them to the
page for the people who use it, the same way it does for your team. Read more in
[In-context on production](/js-sdk/in-context#tolgee-browser-plugin).

Keep the SDK on your website up to date. The plugin loads the in-context dialog in the same
version of `@tolgee/web` as your website uses, so contributors on a website with an older SDK get
a dialog that can't suggest, whatever version of the plugin they have. Suggesting in the dialog
needs `@tolgee/web` 7.3.0 or newer, and
[signing in through the plugin](/js-sdk/in-context#sign-in-with-your-tolgee-account) needs 7.2.0
or newer.

## What Contributors Need

Contributing takes no invitation and no setup on your side for each person. A contributor needs
two things.

- A Tolgee account on the server your project lives on. On Tolgee Cloud, anyone can
  [sign up for free](https://app.tolgee.io/sign_up). On a self-hosted instance, the account comes
  from your server, as described in [Preparing Your Project](#preparing-your-project).
- The Tolgee Tools plugin for
  [Google Chrome](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/tolgee-tools/hacnbapajkkfohnonhbmegojnddagfnj)
  or [Mozilla Firefox](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/tolgee-tools/).

With both in place, the contributor connects the plugin to your project in three steps.

1. Open your website and click the Tolgee Tools icon in the browser toolbar.
2. Choose `Connect to Tolgee`.
3. Sign in and allow the plugin to work with your project on the consent screen.

After that, holding `Alt` and clicking a string on your website opens the dialog. You don't have
to write these steps down yourself: the `Translate with Tolgee` badge described in
[Inviting Contributors with a Badge](#inviting-contributors-with-a-badge) links to a page that
walks a first-time contributor through them.

## Inviting Contributors with a Badge

Someone who notices a clumsy translation on your website has no way of knowing they are allowed
to fix it. A `Translate with Tolgee` badge tells them, and links to
[Translating a Website with Tolgee](../translate-with-tolgee.mdx), a short page for first-time
contributors: install the plugin, sign in, Alt+click the text, and what to do when something
doesn't work.

The badge looks like this.

[![Translate with Tolgee](/img/badges/translate-with-tolgee.svg)](../translate-with-tolgee.mdx)

Put it where people who read your text will see it, in the footer of your website, next to your
language switcher, or in your README.

For a website, paste this HTML.

```html
<a href="https://docs.tolgee.io/translate-with-tolgee" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
  <img src="https://docs.tolgee.io/img/badges/translate-with-tolgee.svg" alt="Translate with Tolgee" height="28">
</a>
```

For a README or anything else that takes Markdown, paste this.

```markdown
[![Translate with Tolgee](https://docs.tolgee.io/img/badges/translate-with-tolgee.svg)](https://docs.tolgee.io/translate-with-tolgee)
```

The badge does its work on the running website, because that is where a contributor can Alt+click
the text. In a README it still tells people the project accepts translations, but they have to
open the website before anything happens.

You can also download the badge and serve it with your own assets, instead of loading it from
`docs.tolgee.io`.

:::caution
The badge sends people to a page that assumes your website is ready for them. Before you publish
it, check that your project is public, that `Suggestions mode` is on, and that your site declares
`apiUrl` and `projectId`. Without those, a contributor follows all three steps and then finds
nothing to click.
:::

## What Contributors Can and Can't Do

In the dialog, a contributor gets the same
[community permissions](./community-translation.mdx#what-contributors-can-do) as in the platform.
The dialog shows only what they can use, so they never face a disabled form.

This is the dialog a contributor sees after changing the Czech text of a button.

<ScreenshotWrapper
  src="/img/docs/platform/community-translation/in-context-suggest-dialog.webp"
  alt="The Quick translation dialog for a user who may only suggest: English and Czech fields each with the note Your change will be sent as a suggestion, two pending Czech suggestions, and the Suggest button"
  style={{ maxWidth: 560 }}
/>

A contributor can

- See the translations of the string in the languages they choose
- See the key's screenshots
- See the suggestions other people have already made
- Suggest a new translation in any language
- Delete their own suggestion, as long as they granted the plugin that permission on the consent
  screen

A contributor can't

- Change a live translation or its [state](./translation_states.mdx)
- Accept or decline suggestions, or delete suggestions made by others
- Create keys, so a string that has no key in your project yet can't receive suggestions
- Edit tags, plural settings, or the character limit, or upload screenshots

## Reviewing the Suggestions

Suggestions made in context are ordinary suggestions. You find them in the translations view, and
you can [review them](./community-translation.mdx#reviewing-community-suggestions) there.

Your team members can also review without leaving your website. With the plugin connected, they
see the pending suggestions under each language in the same dialog and can accept, decline, or
delete them according to their permissions. Read more in
[Reviewing Suggestions in the Dialog](/js-sdk/in-context#reviewing-suggestions-in-the-dialog).

The people who suggested appear in the `Community` tab of the
[Members](/platform/projects_and_organizations/members#community-contributors) page, where you can
invite the most active ones to your team.
